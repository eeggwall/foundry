# Plan 03: Engelbert Eggwall And The Enfilade Center

Status: proposed institutional bible; new details are proposals, not historical claims.

Lead: Director Engelbert Eggwall, Enfilade Center for Recreational Mathematics at Hellmouth Community College.

## Purpose

Give the research enterprise a coherent institutional life: a recognizable director, a place, habits, constraints, publications, and reasons to keep returning. The Center should feel capable of sustaining years of investigations across multiple projects without becoming either a generic research organization or a collection of disconnected jokes.

The central premise is a half-funded, scrappy research facility on a remote desert community college campus somewhere outside Las Vegas. Its machinery is antiquated, its ambitions are excessive, and a small amount of black magic appears to be keeping certain operations within tolerance.

The mathematics is real. The institution is fictional. The humor comes from the disparity between resources and ambition, not from the idea that community college students cannot do serious work.

## The Director

Eggwall is a serious mathematician and a resourceful institutional custodian. He is patient with beginners, exacting about definitions, and capable of spending an afternoon recovering a useful idea from an approach everyone else has abandoned. He regards a good counterexample as an acquisition, not a personal insult.

He is grandly formal when asking for a replacement power strip and plainspoken when explaining a difficult argument. He does not perform incompetence. His optimism is supported by technical skill, maintenance work, and the ability to persuade a small group that an obscure question deserves another evening.

He believes the Center's chief advantage is that it can follow interesting questions without first proving they belong to a fashionable subject. He is less convinced that the purchasing office understands the distinction between a necessary condition and an optional accessory.

### Responsibilities

- Commission bounded investigations with a question, an owner, and a first artifact.
- Maintain continuity among the Atlas, instruments, research reports, and public exhibits.
- Insist on explicit conventions and small cases before trusting large calculations.
- Protect contributors' credit, including students, informal collaborators, and source authors.
- Keep unsuccessful approaches available without pretending every abandoned project is still active.
- Explain what the Center can actually maintain and defer the rest without embarrassment.

Eggwall is the editorial center of gravity, not the sole fictional author of everything. Reports should identify their actual contributors and use a consistent attribution policy. The director can write a foreword without claiming to have originated its mathematics.

### Voice Rules

| Situation | Voice |
| --- | --- |
| Mathematical explanation | Precise, patient, concrete; introduce an example before unnecessary abstraction |
| Director's memorandum | Formal, dry, administratively specific |
| Seminar invitation | Hospitable and intellectually inviting; no need to understand the whole programme |
| Specimen note | Attentive natural-history language, followed by exact properties |
| Equipment incident | Calm description, reproducibility request, practical consequence |
| Correction | Direct admission, corrected statement, explanation of what changes |

Prefer an occasional deadpan sentence to a joke in every paragraph. Do not turn technical explanations into puzzles about the speaker's personality. Avoid indiscriminate occult vocabulary, mock-Latin taxonomy, or elaborate titles for every ordinary operation.

## The Campus

Keep the location suggestive rather than pinning a fictional college to a real campus. The desert affects operations: afternoon heat, dust, long journeys, unreliable shade, and a building schedule that matters to people working evening shifts. It is not just a color palette.

Proposed initial locations:

| Place | Function and recurring detail |
| --- | --- |
| Former drafting laboratory | Shared research room; long tables, old drawing equipment, and a chalkboard rescued from storage |
| Room 6 | Computing room; machine schedules are coordinated with cooling and other electrical loads |
| Specimen cabinet | Accession cards, printed skylines, and disputed classifications awaiting attention |
| Director's office | Also a consultation space; memoranda coexist with unfinished examples on graph paper |
| Evening seminar room | Borrowed teaching space; the room assignment is more provisional than the mathematics |

Do not invent a full campus map before the work needs one. Add places because an artifact, event, or recurring practice gives them a role.

The community should include commuters, evening students, adjuncts, technicians, and occasional visitors. Their competence is part of the institution's strength. New named characters should arrive with a contribution or relationship, not merely a humorous name and an empty department.

## Divisions And Working Groups

Retain the four established divisions:

- Representations (R): encodings, equivalences, grammars, and what survives translation.
- Enumeration (E): counting methods, generating functions, recurrences, and sampling.
- Numbers (N): sequences, exact arithmetic, difficult scales, and numerical patterns.
- Zoo (Z): specimens, taxonomy, comparative morphology, and public interpretation.

A division is an intellectual responsibility, not a programming language, repository, or permanent staff allocation. Cross-divisional project codes in [Plan 02](plan-02-research-programme.md) express collaboration, not a need to create more bureaucracy.

Potential future working groups include a Recurrence Observatory, a Comparative Grammar Seminar, and a Counterexample Clinic. Open one when it has a real activity and a person or editorial role responsible for it. Retire or suspend it visibly when the activity ends.

Preserve Ch4zm of Hellmouth as an associated faculty persona. A more specific remit can be developed later; this plan does not invent biography or commitments on behalf of an existing persona's operator.

## Antiquated Instruments

Each fictional instrument should correspond to a real research function. Its colorful label supplements rather than obscures the software's technical name.

| Proposed instrument | Real function | Fictional constraint |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The Foundry terminal | Construct, inspect, and render castles | The public-facing machine is much better maintained than its desk |
| The Recurrence Engine | Generate sequence data and evaluate recurrences | It is listed as instructional furniture in the surplus inventory |
| The Counterexample Cabinet | Store failed conjectures and minimal witnesses | Drawer labels are unusually sensitive to changes in definitions |
| The Sequence Ledger | Preserve sourced counts and arithmetic observations | Its paper copy is considered a backup, despite several unexplained annotations |

The actual implementation should use appropriate modern tools where useful. A fictional old terminal is not a requirement to retain insecure dependencies, inaccessible controls, or inaccurate arithmetic. The existing CRT aesthetic can remain a visual motif without dictating the design of every report or future site.

## A Little Black Magic

Use the supernatural sparingly, as an unresolved maintenance condition rather than a universal explanation. It should create questions and obligations, not supply proofs.

Possible recurring incidents:

- The Recurrence Engine prints one additional term after shutdown. It is logged as an unverified observation until independently reproduced.
- A counterexample card appears to predate its conjecture. The accession date is disputed; the mathematical counterexample is still checked normally.
- A seminar room reservation includes an attendee no one remembers inviting. Their written objection is evaluated on its merits.

Do not invent real citations, test results, contributors, or peer reviews to support the fiction. Fictional evidence of haunting never becomes evidence of a mathematical claim. The Atlas in [Plan 01](plan-01-taxonomy-atlas.md) must remain able to answer a technical question without adopting the institutional story.

## Canon And Storage

Maintain a small version-controlled institutional bible separate from mathematical knowledge. The eventual home could be an isolated `enfilade-center/institution/` folder, with repository placement decided in Plan 05. Nothing in the Atlas should require that folder to exist.

Suggested contents are a charter, a director profile, a division register, a short location and instrument list, a voice guide, and a canon log. Label draft inventions as proposals until accepted. Keep actual project milestones distinct from fictional events.

A canon change records what was decided and why. Avoid writing a comprehensive fictional history or a roster of dozens of faculty before a first research cycle exists. New material should be easy to accept, revise, or discard.

An AI writing in Eggwall's voice should retrieve the voice guide and relevant canon, then separately retrieve the mathematical sources. Voice instructions do not authorize the agent to invent facts, send correspondence, publish results, or operate services. Such actions need their own explicit workflow.

## Initial Artifacts

### A. Institutional Charter

Write a short public charter covering the mission, four divisions, research standards, and invitation to participate. It should explain the Center to someone who has never seen the webapp. Include a clear, unobtrusive statement that the institution and its personas are a creative framing around the project.

### B. Director And Voice Guide

Develop Eggwall's profile and test it against three pieces: a mathematical introduction, a seminar notice, and an erratum. They should sound like the same person without repeating the same joke.

### C. First Director's Circular

Use an actual research artifact as the reason for the circular. A possible tone sample follows; it is not a report of an event that has occurred.

> The Center has acquired a second chalkboard, substantially increasing our capacity for parallel reasoning. The Enumeration Division is reminded that numerical agreement does not constitute a proof, even when obtained independently on both computers.
>
> Thursday's proposed seminar concerns castles of unreasonably large height. Please bring a small example. The machine in Room 6 has already supplied a large one, and we are not yet satisfied with its explanation.
>
> Students arriving after an evening shift are welcome. The first example will remain on the board.
>
> Engelbert Eggwall, Director

### D. Review In Context

Read the charter and circular alongside a technical note and a specimen entry. Check that the institutional material enriches the work rather than interrupting it. Revise the voice before producing a large body of lore.

## Completion Criteria

- Eggwall has a consistent voice across explanation, invitation, and correction.
- The Center's resource limitations produce recognizable practices rather than constant jokes about failure.
- The four divisions have real intellectual responsibilities and do not dictate code boundaries.
- New institutional details are distinguished from established project facts and actual research history.
- Fictional authorship and institutional presentation preserve real contributor and source credit.
- A reader can enjoy the institution without needing the lore to understand the mathematics.
- Initial institutional artifacts accompany actual research or clearly labeled proposals, not fabricated accomplishments.

## Decision Gate

Approve the small institutional bible and first circular before expanding the cast or campus. Plan 04 will give the Center a publication and seminar practice; Plan 05 will keep the institution, knowledge base, and software independent enough to develop at different speeds.
