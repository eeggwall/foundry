ABOUT THIS SITE

The Enfilade Center studies castles: flat-topped stacks of blocks sitting on a full-width base. A block is a maximal horizontal run inside a row, and the base counts as one of them. Project Euler problem 502 asks how many castles of a given size have an even number of blocks. We started there and did not stop there.

This site is the index, not the work. Reports, registry, datasets and exhibits live in the places listed below; this page exists so that you can find them without knowing which of us made them. Where a document is a draft or a proposal, it says so in the status column.

Divisions: Representations (R), Enumeration (E), Numbers (N), Zoo (Z). Director: Engelbert Eggwall. Funding: approximately half.

SUBMIT / VISIT

Contributions are accepted as reviewable patches to the Atlas. Attach a proof, a reproducible experiment, or a plain statement of what is still unknown. A computational observation is welcome. A computational observation labelled as a theorem is not.

Seminar meets Thursday evenings in borrowed teaching space. The room assignment is posted Wednesday and is more provisional than the mathematics. Commuters, evening students, adjuncts and technicians are all welcome. Bring a small example.

Machine time in Room 6 is scheduled around the building's cooling load. Afternoon requests will be moved to evening without further discussion.

DIVISIONS
R — Representations
Encodings, equivalences, grammars, and what survives translation.
E — Enumeration
Counting methods, generating functions, recurrences, sampling.
N — Numbers
Sequences, exact arithmetic, difficult scales, numerical patterns.
Z — Zoo
Specimens, taxonomy, comparative morphology, public interpretation.

A division is a way of asking questions. It is not a repository, a language, or a staffing line. A code like E/N-02 means two divisions are working together, not that a third has been created.

The Center has acquired a second chalkboard, substantially increasing our capacity for parallel reasoning. The Enumeration Division is reminded that numerical agreement does not constitute a proof, even when obtained independently on both computers.

Thursday's proposed seminar concerns castles of unreasonably large height. Please bring a small example. The machine in Room 6 has already supplied a large one, and we are not yet satisfied with its explanation.

Students arriving after an evening shift are welcome. The first example will remain on the board.
